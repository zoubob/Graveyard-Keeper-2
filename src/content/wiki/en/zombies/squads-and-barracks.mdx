## What Are Zombie Squads in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Zombie squads are the armed layer of your undead operation. Your worker zombies keep the economy running; your squad zombies march where you point them. The [official Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/) states the pitch directly: you lead zombies into battle, build towers and fortifications, craft armor and weapons, and train troops to save the realm from a zombie apocalypse.

That wording maps cleanly onto four systems working together:

- **Barracks** organize individual zombies into squads.
- **Crafting** produces the armor and weapons your troops carry.
- **Towers and fortifications** give your forces somewhere to stand and something to defend.
- **You** lead the army, because leadership is the one input no zombie provides.

Dedicated guide coverage dedicates a chapter to the whole loop — "Zombie Squads: Barracks, Pikes & Bows" — which is the most detailed public documentation available right now. The rest of this page turns that outline into a workable plan.

## How Does the Barracks Work?

The barracks is the structure that turns loose zombies into organized troops, according to dedicated community zombie-guide coverage. Zombies assigned there train and equip as soldiers rather than haulers, with pikes and bows named as the two weapon types.

Beyond that, public information is thin: no roster sizes, no training times, no per-weapon stats have been documented as of the v1.0 launch. What survives as practical guidance is the economics. Every soldier is a worker you pulled out of production, so the decision to form a squad is really a decision about your corpse pipeline. The order most guides imply is sensible on its face: stable logistics network first, barracks second. If you are still feeding your Supply Station by hand, the army can wait — the [zombie logistics guide](/zombies/logistics-network/) covers getting production self-sufficient first.

## Pike or Bow Zombies: Which Should You Train?

Honest answer: nobody credible is ranking them yet. Community documentation confirms both troop types exist and stops there — no damage values, no range numbers, no tier list worth trusting has been published.

The one piece of advanced advice that recurs is directional: **diversify your army instead of fielding one unit type.** That advice is standard in any game with mixed troop roles, and it holds up here even without numbers, because a mixed force stays usable when you hit a fight that punishes a single-minded composition.

A sensible starting split, framed as community guidance rather than doctrine:

1. Train some of each weapon type early so you own the comparison.
2. Watch which troops carry their weight in the battles you actually fight.
3. Reinvest in the mix that performs, keeping at least a secondary presence of the other.

That way the question answers itself with evidence from your own save instead of somebody else's guess.

## How Do Squads Fit the Town Defense Loop?

The town runs its own defense layer, and it is worth keeping the two systems straight in your head. Community town-guide coverage describes night guards who handle local security after dark — a town-internal structure with its own rhythm. Your squads are different: they are the force you personally lead out, backed by the towers and fortifications the official description names.

The practical relationship is a loop rather than a rivalry. A well-defended town survives its nights, which keeps the economy ticking, which funds more equipment and more fortifications, which makes your expeditions stronger. Both halves consume resources and both depend on the same underlying income, which is why the [town rebuilding guide](/guides/town-rebuilding/) and your barracks plans belong on the same page of your notebook. New players still finding their footing can get the broader context from the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) before committing corpses to the military track.

## How Many Troops Do You Need?

No public source gives a number, and any figure quoted in a comment section is a guess. What the available guidance supports is a shape for the decision:

- **Start smaller than you want.** A compact, fully equipped squad beats a horde draining gear and food you cannot spare.
- **Scale with threats, not with pride.** Expand when you meet a fight your current force cannot handle, not before.
- **Remember the horde penalty.** Guide coverage on zombies generally notes that oversized hordes carry penalties, and there is no reason to assume your army is exempt from good sense.

The [main zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) covers the broader workforce trade-offs — wages you never pay, capacity you always pay for — and the same arithmetic applies to soldiers.

## What Should You Build Before Your First Big Battle?

Fortifications and equipment come before troop count. The official description puts crafting armor and weapons and building towers on equal footing with training troops, and the logic is straightforward: unarmored zombies fall, and every corpse you lose on a battlefield is a corpse your economy does not get back. Prepare the gear, place your defenses, walk your supply lines, and only then march on the scale that feels ambitious.
