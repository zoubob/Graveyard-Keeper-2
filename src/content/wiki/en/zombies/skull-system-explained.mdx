## What Are White and Red Skulls in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Every corpse carries two separate ratings: white skulls measure burial quality, and red skulls measure zombie potential. A body rich in white skulls is an asset to your graveyard; a body rich in red skulls is an asset to your workforce. This two-currency split is the most corroborated system in the current community materials — four independent guides describe it the same way — which makes it the right mechanic to learn first.

The distinction matters because corpses are your scarcest serious resource. Every body that arrives at your yard can serve one of those futures first, and reading its skulls before you commit it is the difference between compounding progress and quietly wasting your best inputs.

| Rating | Measures | Best used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White skulls | Graveyard and burial quality | Burials, cemetery rating |
| Red skulls | Zombie potential | Workers, elite builds, squads |

## What Do White Skulls Affect?

White skulls are the graveyard currency. They describe how good a corpse is for burial, and burials built on high-white-skull bodies are what push your cemetery quality upward. Community guides consistently frame white-skull corpses as the ones you want in the ground rather than on the workbench.

Cemetery quality itself is maximized by burial decorations rather than by bodies alone — the [items overview](/items/getting-started/) covers the gear involved in making graves presentable. So the white-skull loop has two halves: pick bodies that rate well for burial, then surround them with the decorations that convert that rating into real cemetery quality. Players who skip the second half often blame the corpses for a yard that underperforms, when the missing piece was the dressing around them.

## What Do Red Skulls Affect?

Red skulls are the workforce currency. They measure a corpse's potential as a zombie, which makes them the number to check before you commit a body to the embalming table. The higher the red-skull rating, the more ceiling the finished worker has, and community guides treat red skulls as the driving quality metric for the entire zombie system.

The practical consequence is that two corpses that look identical at the cart drop-off can be entirely different assets. One belongs in a grave; the other belongs on an operating table. The [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) covers how red skulls translate into stats, organs and elite workers, so this page stays focused on reading the ratings correctly before you spend a body at all.

## How Do You Move a Corpse From White-Skull to Red-Skull Use?

The embalming table is where you prepare bodies, and community guides describe body shaping there as the way to modify skull ratings before a corpse is committed to one future or the other. In plain terms, you are not stuck with the ratings a body arrives with — but changing them costs preparation work, so decide the corpse's role before you touch the table rather than after.

Two community-reported notes belong here. First, shaping is described as a deliberate pre-raise step in the zombie workflow, which is why hasty corpses produce disappointing workers. Second, a tips video recommends vegetable salad before surgeries — feeding the keeper the right food ahead of delicate work for flawless outcomes. That tip comes from player experience rather than documentation, so hold it loosely and test it in your own game. No exact formulas or point values for any of this are public as of the v1.0 launch; anyone quoting precise numbers is extrapolating.

## How Many Red Skulls Can a Zombie Have?

The community target is five red skulls — guides describe five-red-skull zombies plus body shaping as the recipe for elite workers capable of running demanding stations alone. What is not publicly confirmed is the hard maximum, or the exact math of how shaping and organs interact with the rating.

That gap is worth respecting. Because no formula has been published, the reliable approach is directional: push red skulls upward with shaping before raising, then invest organs afterward. If a finished zombie underperforms, the shortfall usually traces back to the corpse you started from rather than the organs you added. Five red skulls is a target the community converged on, not a number the game advertises, so plan around the direction rather than the digit.

## Should You Bury or Recycle Every Corpse?

Neither, by default — sort them. The decision framework community guides converge on looks like this:

1. **Read both ratings first.** A corpse is a white-skull asset or a red-skull asset depending on its numbers, not its appearance.
2. **Feed the graveyard what it needs.** While you are still pushing [cemetery quality](/items/cemetery-quality/), high-white-skull bodies go in the ground with proper decorations.
3. **Feed the workforce the rest.** High-red-skull bodies go to the embalming table, especially once you need porters and machine operators.
4. **Revisit as priorities shift.** Early on the graveyard pays; later the automation pays. Other income streams are covered in the [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide/).

The mistake to avoid is treating every corpse the same way out of habit. A high-white-skull body wasted on manual labor, or a high-red-skull body buried out of routine, is the same error in different clothing: spending your best resource on your least urgent need. New players working through the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) can simply default to burials at first, then graduate to sorting as the zombie system opens up.
