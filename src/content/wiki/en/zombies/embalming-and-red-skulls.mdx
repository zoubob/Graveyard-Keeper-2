## What Does the Embalming Table Do?

The embalming table is the workshop where corpses are prepared for reanimation. Every step that turns a delivered body into a capable worker passes across it: organs are harvested, the body can be shaped, and its skull ratings get modified before you commit it to the graveyard or the workforce. Dedicated guide coverage treats the table as the single most important station in Graveyard Keeper 2's zombie pipeline, because it is where a corpse's ceiling gets set.

One clarification before going further. The [skull system explainer](/zombies/skull-system-explained/) on this site answers the *what* — what white and red skulls measure, and whether a body should be buried or recycled. This page is the *how*: the hands-on craft of taking a corpse you have decided to turn into a worker and making that worker as good as the body allows.

## How Do Organs Affect Zombie Stats?

Organs are the improvement currency. Community guide coverage dedicates an entire chapter to "Improving Your Zombies: Organs & Stats," and the short version is that organs harvested during preparation drive the stats of the zombie you raise. Better organ work in, better worker out.

The practical loop, as guides present it:

1. **Harvest during preparation.** Work the corpse at the embalming table and set aside the organs relevant to the worker you want.
2. **Raise the zombie.** The finished worker starts with stats reflecting the body you built it from.
3. **Invest organs into the finished worker.** Push its stats toward the ceiling the body allows.

What no public source provides is the math — no per-organ values, no formulas, no exact stat curves have been documented as of the v1.0 launch. So treat organ investment as a direction rather than a recipe: bodies you care about get careful organ work, bodies you do not care about get none.

## What Is Body Shaping?

Body shaping is the other half of the craft. Guide coverage pairs the embalming table with red skulls in a dedicated chapter and describes "shaping" the body as the way you modify its skull ratings before reanimation. In plain terms: the rating a corpse arrives with is not necessarily the rating it keeps. Preparation can move a body toward the rating profile you want it to have.

Advanced community tips compress this into a phrase worth remembering — "5 red skull zombies + shaping bodies." The pairing is deliberate. Shaping is how players reach the top of the rating scale, and the top of the rating scale is where the best workers come from. Exact point values and per-action effects remain undocumented, so experiment on disposable corpses before you commit prize specimens.

## How Do You Craft a 5-Red-Skull Zombie?

The five-red-skull zombie is the community's benchmark for an elite worker, and the build path the guides describe is a sequence rather than a secret:

1. **Start from the best corpse available.** Shaping modifies ratings, but it does not invent quality from nothing — a strong starting body does most of the work.
2. **Shape the body at the embalming table** to push its red skull rating as high as it will go.
3. **Invest organs** into the raised worker to develop its stats toward the ceiling.
4. **Give it a job that matters.** An elite worker is a scarce resource; community players spend them on demanding production stations rather than basic hauling, which porter zombies handle fine.

Because none of the underlying numbers are public, treat any specific build order you see — including this one — as community-reported. The reliable part is the direction: better body in, better worker out.

## Does Food Quality Matter Before Surgery?

One community tips video reports a curious detail: feeding a vegetable salad before surgery supposedly enables flawless operations. It is repeated as a tip, not as a rule, and no patch notes or documentation confirm it.

That makes it exactly the kind of claim worth testing yourself. The cost of a salad is trivial, and if it works, you will see it in your own results. If it does nothing, you have lost nothing. Until it is verified either way, hold it loosely — the honest answer is "players report it works; try it and see." Broader food and upkeep questions for living workers are a different system entirely, covered in the [items getting-started guide](/items/getting-started/).

## Embalming vs Straight Burial: Which Corpses Go Where?

Every corpse has a best use, and the embalming table is also where you decide what that use is:

- **Burial candidates.** Bodies with strong white skull ratings serve the graveyard, where burial quality drives your rating. Some may still deserve light preparation before interment.
- **Worker candidates.** Bodies with red skull potential go through full preparation — shaping plus organs — and become zombies; the creation basics live in the [main zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/).
- **Neither.** Weak or damaged bodies that serve no role are cremation fuel, a decision covered in the [corpse management guide](/guides/corpse-management/).

The habit that separates tidy operations from chaotic ones is making this call the moment a body arrives, not after it has been sitting in a pile. And once a shaped, organ-invested worker is on its feet, keep developing it — perks, experience and the mastery track covered in the [zombie progression guide](/zombies/gardening-and-perks/) are the layers that come after surgery.
