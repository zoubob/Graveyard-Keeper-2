## How Do Zombies Work in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Zombies are worker units you build from corpses instead of hiring. A Supply Station keeps them fed and equipped, red skulls and organs decide how capable each one is, and barracks organize them into squads. Build a focused workforce rather than a huge horde, because oversized hordes carry penalties.

The [official Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/) confirms the pitch — you command an undead army and automate production — and community guides treat the zombie system as the backbone of mid-game industry. Four systems interlock:

- **Creation and quality.** You craft zombies from corpses, and their potential is measured in red skulls.
- **Upkeep.** A Supply Station keeps every worker fed and equipped, so it keeps working without you.
- **Growth.** Zombies gain perks and experience, and a separate Zombie Mastery track gates how strong your workforce can become.
- **Organization.** Barracks turn workers into squads, and community guides report that horde size itself carries trade-offs.

The sections below walk each layer in order.

## How Do You Make a Zombie?

You make a zombie from a corpse. Prepare the body, raise it as a worker, and it follows your production plan instead of rotting in a pile. The embalming table sits at the center of the process, because it is also where quality gets settled.

The pipeline, as the [dedicated community zombie guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R1SfY1jXYgw) lays it out:

1. **Get a corpse.** Any body works, but not every body is worth the same.
2. **Prepare it at the embalming table.** Red skull ratings and body shaping are handled here.
3. **Raise it.** The finished zombie joins your workforce and draws food and tools from your Supply Station.
4. **Improve it.** Organs and further work push its stats toward what the body allows.

Community videos also cover a block they label Zombie Gardening, built around stands, shovels and tablets. The chapter structure is clear; the exact interactions are community-reported, so verify them in your own game before planning an economy around them.

## What Do Red Skulls and Organs Do to Zombie Stats?

Red skulls measure a corpse's potential as a zombie, and organs are what you spend to push a raised zombie toward that ceiling. Community guides describe shaping the body first, then improving the finished worker with organs, with elite builds starting from the highest-rated corpses available.

| Input | What the community says it does |
| --- | --- |
| Red skulls on the corpse | Set the quality ceiling of the finished zombie |
| Organs | Improve the stats of an existing zombie |
| Body shaping | Modifies skull ratings before you raise the body |
| Perks and experience | Accumulate as the zombie works |

The headline target among players is the elite worker: a five-red-skull corpse plus careful body shaping yields a zombie that can run demanding stations unsupervised. Perks and experience then stack on top as the worker puts in shifts, which is why guides advise keeping good zombies. For the full two-currency model — including why a corpse that is poor for burials can be ideal for your factory — read the [skull system explainer](/zombies/skull-system-explained/). When you're ready to act on it, the [embalming walkthrough](/zombies/embalming-and-red-skulls/) covers the table-to-elite-worker process step by step.

## How Does the Supply Station Keep Zombies Working?

The Supply Station keeps your zombies fed and equipped, and it is the difference between a workforce that runs forever and one that stalls. Community guides recommend giving workers bronze-tier or better tools so they keep pace with production demands.

Two roles matter beyond basic upkeep. Zombies excel as a porter network — community tips describe dedicating them purely to logistics, hauling goods between stations so you never carry anything yourself. Players also report running factory-scale automation once the supply loop is stable, with zombies feeding machines from raw inputs to finished goods. Setup advice from the same guides:

- Place supply infrastructure close to the stations your zombies serve.
- Upgrade tools before adding more bodies.
- Treat food and equipment as production goals, same as sellable goods.

For the full hauler setup — placement, tooling and the path to factory automation — see the [logistics network guide](/zombies/logistics-network/). As you work through the [town rebuilding checklist](/guides/town-rebuilding/), this is the system quietly handling your material runs.

## What Are Zombie Squads and the Barracks?

The barracks turns your workforce into an army. Community guides describe building a barracks, organizing zombies into squads, and arming them along weapon types that include pike and bow lines — the command-an-undead-army layer the official description promises, sitting on top of the worker layer.

Squad sizes and combat specifics are not documented anywhere official, so treat the community videos as the best current source. For planning purposes, one implication matters more than any detail: every fighter you raise is a porter or crafter you did not raise, so squads are a second claim on your corpse supply. Decide early what share of your bodies feeds the barracks versus the production floor, and revisit that split as your economy matures — the [squads and barracks guide](/zombies/squads-and-barracks/) walks through that decision in detail.

## Is There a Limit to How Many Zombies You Should Have?

Yes — community consensus says too many zombies is a debuff. Oversized hordes incur penalties, so the strongest workforce is focused rather than massive. No exact threshold has been published, so treat any hard number as unverified.

The rule of thumb that falls out of the guides: add a zombie only when you can answer yes to three questions. Does the Supply Station cover one more worker? Is there a real job waiting for it? Are you comfortably below the point where penalties start? If any answer is no, invest in tools or organs instead of bodies. Quality compounds across [perks, experience and mastery](/zombies/gardening-and-perks/); quantity eventually taxes you.

## Where Should a New Player Start With Zombies?

Start small and start early. Raise your first zombie from an ordinary corpse, put it on hauling duty, and let the Supply Station absorb the upkeep. Learn the skull system before chasing elite builds, because your early bodies teach the loop cheaply.

A sensible opening plan:

1. Follow the early routines in the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) until corpses are flowing, then sort every delivery with the [corpse management routine](/guides/corpse-management/).
2. Raise one zombie and assign it to logistics; keep its supply line stocked.
3. Read the skull system explainer before spending organs, so you know which corpses deserve them.
4. Check the [items overview](/items/getting-started/) for what your graveyard and workshops realistically need next.

From there, the [guides hub](/guides/) maps the rest of the game's systems. Zombies reward patience: a small, well-fed, well-equipped workforce beats a shambling mob in every way the game measures.
