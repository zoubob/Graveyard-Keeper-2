## What Is Zombie Gardening in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Zombie gardening is the name one dedicated community zombie video gives to a cluster of late-game zombie tasks, in a chapter titled "Zombie Gardening: Stand, Shovel & Tablets." The label suggests gardening-style work performed by your undead workforce rather than by you, built around three named pieces: a stand, shovel-like tools, and tablets.

Beyond those names, caution is due. The exact interactions — what the stand does, how the shovel-type tools factor in, what tablets are consumed or produced, whether the output feeds your economy or the zombie system itself — are not publicly documented as of the v1.0 launch. This page will not invent mechanics to fill the gap. What it can do honestly is place gardening in the system map, so when you unlock it in your own save, you know what you are looking at and what to test first.

If you want the wider zombie picture first — creation, upkeep, squads — the [main zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) is the hub page this one sits under.

## How Do Zombie Perks and Experience Work?

The same video dedicates a chapter to "Zombie Perks & Experience," and the premise is exactly what it sounds like: your zombies gain perks and experience over time. That turns each worker from a static tool into something that develops.

The natural reading — flagged as an interpretation, not documented fact — is that experience accumulates as a zombie performs its job, and perks arrive along the way, nudging the worker toward competence at what it already does. If that holds in your save, two habits follow:

- **Keep good workers working.** A developed zombie is an asset you cannot quickly replace; reshuffling your whole roster casually throws away accumulated growth.
- **Match the job to the history.** A porter with long experience hauling between two stations is probably worth more on that route than on a new one.

What is not public: the perk list, experience thresholds, or how much any of it matters numerically. Verify with your own roster before rebuilding your economy around assumed gains.

## What Are Zombie Mastery Levels?

The third chapter, "Zombie Mastery Levels Explained," describes a mastery system that gates zombie potential. In the shape the coverage implies, mastery is an account-level or progression-level layer above individual workers — your broader zombie-handling skill rising as you engage with the system, determining how strong your workforce can become.

Mastery, perks and organs stack in that order of permanence:

| Layer | Scope | Documented? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organs and shaping | The individual corpse | Named in dedicated guide coverage; values undocumented |
| Perks and experience | The individual worker over time | Named as a system; details undocumented |
| Mastery levels | Your overall zombie progression | Named as a system; details undocumented |

The closest analogy on this site is the player-side progression covered in the [tech points and mastery guide](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) — a ceiling you raise through engagement rather than a switch you flip. Treat the zombie version as real but opaque: the layer exists, the numbers do not.

## How Should You Prioritize Zombie Progression?

With three progression layers and no published math, the pragmatic order community guidance supports is this:

1. **Get bodies right first.** Shaping and organ investment set each worker's ceiling — see the [embalming and red skulls guide](/zombies/embalming-and-red-skulls/) for that craft. No amount of experience fixes a weak body.
2. **Let experience accumulate.** Give developed workers stable jobs so perks accrue rather than reset.
3. **Engage mastery as it unlocks.** Whatever raises mastery, doing more of the zombie system does it — which makes ordinary play the cheapest investment.
4. **Fold gardening in last.** Until its interactions are verified, treat gardening as an experiment slot, not an economy pillar.

That ordering errs toward the layers with confirmed existence and defers the layer with the least documentation.

## What Is Still Unverified?

An honesty section, because this page draws on a single source:

- **The stand's function.** Named in the gardening chapter; behavior undocumented.
- **Shovel-type tools.** Named; how they are used or consumed is not.
- **Tablets.** Named; whether they are inputs, outputs, or progression items is not.
- **Perk specifics.** The system is named; no perk list or effect sizes are public.
- **Mastery thresholds.** The gate exists per coverage; what raises it, and by how much, does not.
- **Horde penalty details.** "Too many zombies is a debuff" is reported; which penalties apply at which sizes is not.

None of this means the systems are imaginary — the chapter structure is a real, dedicated walkthrough of the game. It means the safe posture is testing in your own save, with the [logistics network guide](/zombies/logistics-network/) as the place your findings plug into a working economy.
