## What Is a Zombie Logistics Network?

A zombie logistics network is what happens when individual zombie workers stop being a novelty and start being infrastructure. Instead of one zombie hauling one crate, you design a connected system: porters pull raw materials from storage, stations process them, and finished goods flow onward without you touching a single item. Advanced guide coverage consistently names this as the number one advanced tip in Graveyard Keeper 2 — build a zombie logistics network — because it converts a steady corpse supply into passive industrial output.

It helps to think of the network in three layers:

- **Porters** move items between storage, stations and stockpiles.
- **Production stations** transform those items, staffed by zombies with the right tools.
- **The Supply Station** keeps every worker fed and equipped so the chain never stalls.

Your role shifts from doing the work to designing the flow — and hunting down the choke points.

## Why Are Zombies the Best Workers in the Game?

Community coverage is blunt about the appeal: zombies draw no wages and, as guides describe them, they never tire. Every hour a zombie spends hauling goods is an hour you can spend on the graveyard, the town, or anything that actually needs a human brain. The [official Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/) lists automating production with expanded machinery as a core pillar of the game, and guide chapters describe zombies operating factories as the intended end state.

The honest caveat is that "best" does not mean "effortless." Zombies need setup: tools, food, and a corpse pipeline to expand capacity. Until the network hums, you are the workforce. After it hums, you are management. That trade — heavy setup for zero-cost labor — is why beginner coverage recommends allocating zombies as porters early, even before any factory vision exists. For the fundamentals of creating workers, start with the [main zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/).

## What Do You Need Before Building the Network?

Three prerequisites come up in every walkthrough:

1. **A working corpse pipeline.** Zombies come from corpses, and a growing network consumes them fast. Decide early which bodies serve the graveyard and which serve the factory — the [skull system explainer](/zombies/skull-system-explained/) covers that split in detail.
2. **Tools at bronze tier or better.** Community guides treat bronze-tier or higher equipment on zombies as the prerequisite for useful labor. An under-equipped zombie is a body occupying a job slot, not a worker filling it.
3. **A stocked Supply Station.** Dedicated zombie-guide coverage describes the Supply Station as the structure that keeps working zombies fed and equipped. If it runs dry, the network degrades quietly — items stop moving while your workforce stands around, fully employed and fully idle.

Set those three legs in place before optimizing anything else.

## How Do You Set Up Porter Zombies?

Porters are the recommended entry point, and the setup loop is short:

1. Raise a zombie from a usable corpse.
2. Assign it to porter duty so it ferries goods along a route you designate.
3. Equip it properly, then watch it complete at least one full trip.

The third step matters more than it sounds. A porter with the wrong tool or an ambiguous route can idle indefinitely, and because zombies do not complain, nothing alerts you except a stockpile that refuses to shrink. Check outputs, not intentions: if raw materials pile up in front of a station, the broken link is usually one stop upstream. Start with a small crew covering your busiest route, confirm the loop is stable, and only then extend the web.

## How Does the Network Scale Into Factory Automation?

Scaling is where guide coverage gets enthusiastic. The end state — described in one walkthrough's chapters as zombies now operating factories — is production lines where zombies staff the machines and porters shuttle materials between them, producing while you sleep, quest, or tidy the cemetery.

The reliable path there is incremental:

- Automate one product family end to end before starting a second.
- Place porter routes where goods naturally flow, and shorten any route that crosses half the map.
- Upgrade station tooling as better tiers unlock, because worker output tracks equipment quality.
- Feed expansion from the corpse pipeline, reserving high-potential bodies for the jobs that matter most.

Done well, the network compounds: more automation funds more machinery, machinery absorbs more zombies, and zombies free more of your day. Pair the income side with the [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide/) so factory output actually lands somewhere profitable.

## What Mistakes Break a Zombie Logistics Chain?

The same failure modes recur across community reports:

- **Under-equipping.** Skipping the bronze-tool baseline produces zombies that hold jobs without doing them.
- **Starving the Supply Station.** One empty food or tool slot stalls every worker that depends on it.
- **Building the horde before the workflow.** Zombies beyond your station capacity are upkeep, not output — and oversized hordes carry penalties of their own.
- **Ignoring corpse quality.** A network built on random bodies plateaus fast. Preparation and organ work, covered in the [embalming guide](/zombies/embalming-and-red-skulls/), is how workers reach the potential your stations need.
- **No diagnosis habit.** When output dips, walk the chain from supply to station and fix the first idle link you find.

Avoid those five and the network mostly manages itself — which is the entire point.
