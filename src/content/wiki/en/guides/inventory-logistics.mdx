## How Does Storage Work in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Storage in Graveyard Keeper 2 is not one warehouse with a search bar. Each zone keeps its own inventory, and what you can reach is decided by where you are standing. Community beginner coverage flags this early because it quietly rewrites how you plan a base: a chest built beside the garden cannot be opened from the yard, and no amount of walking between the two will merge them.

The practical consequence is that "where do I put this" is never a global question. It is always local — what does this room do, what does it consume, and what does it produce. Answer those three and the chest layout mostly writes itself.

Two other constraints push in the same direction. Items come in far more varieties than the first game's opening hours suggest, and stack sizes are limited, so a raw pile of everything does not stay tidy for long. Once both facts land, storage stops being a chore you do and becomes a layout you design.

## Why Do Chests Next to Workstations Matter?

The single most repeated piece of storage advice in community coverage is to put a chest next to every workstation you actually use. The reasoning is behavioral rather than mathematical: a chest within arm's reach gets used, and a chest across the map gets bypassed until your pockets are full and production has already stalled.

Coverage also treats container technology as an unlock worth prioritizing. Better chests arrive through the tech tree, and the upgrade compounds — more slots per chest means fewer chests to remember, which means fewer places for a stack to hide. Pair that with the [tech and mastery guide](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) if you are deciding what to research first, and check the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) for where storage sits in the opening order.

The simple-chest technology itself is described as an early unlock, which is the tell that the game expects you to spread containers across the map rather than consolidate them. Treat the chest as a per-room fixture, not a furniture upgrade.

## How Should You Sort Your Chests?

Sort by production process, not by material category. Community coverage calls this organizing by industry, and it is a better rule than it sounds.

Here is the method:

1. Pick a room and read the recipes it runs.
2. List the inputs those recipes consume.
3. Stock its chest with exactly those inputs.
4. Keep outputs in a chest on the other side of the station.

| Approach | What it produces | How it ages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sort by material type (all ore together, all wood together) | A tidy-looking vault | Breaks the moment two rooms share a material |
| Sort by production process (this room's inputs at this room's chest) | A working supply line | Survives expansion, because each room is self-contained |

The process-first scheme wins because it matches how you actually play. You are rarely thinking "I need iron"; you are thinking "the forge is idle." A chest organized around the forge answers the question you asked.

## What Is the Leave-One-Item Habit?

When a chest sits directly beside a station, materials move in and out of it as production runs, and the shelf order drifts. The community habit that counters this is small and effective: leave one unit of a resource in the chest rather than emptying a stack completely.

With one unit still present, matching items have a slot to return to instead of scattering into whichever space opens first. The chest stays legible without you sorting it, and you stop re-learning your own layout every session. It is not a documented game mechanic — it is a bookkeeping habit reported by players, and it costs nothing to adopt.

## How Does Player Inventory Differ From Zombie Logistics?

Keep these two systems separate in your head, because they solve different problems and are documented on different pages.

Your own inventory is about **what you are holding** while you work. The [zombie logistics network](/zombies/logistics-network/) is about **what moves without you** once porters and stations take over. The first is a pocket-management problem; the second is a routing problem.

There is one genuine overlap worth knowing: the zombie network obeys the same zone rule your pockets do. Community coverage of zombie transporter stations notes that a logistics setup only works within a single work zone — the backyard zombie will not fetch from the kitchen. That is the same structural fact showing up twice, which is a good sign you have understood it.

If you are still running your base by hand, solve your own sorting first. A logistics network built on top of chaos just automates the chaos. When you are ready to hand hauling over to the undead, the [main zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) covers the workforce itself.

## What Should You Stop Doing?

A short list of habits that keep storage broken:

- **Building one giant central vault.** It contradicts the per-zone rule and guarantees walking.
- **Sorting by material type.** It looks organized and fails on the second shared resource.
- **Emptying stacks completely.** You lose the return slot and inherit a reshuffled chest.
- **Skipping the chest unlock.** Walking materials between rooms by hand is the chore the tech exists to delete.
- **Carrying everything.** Emptying your pockets before a gathering run is better than discovering the limit mid-route — the [resource guide](/items/resources-and-selling/) covers what is worth hauling home.
