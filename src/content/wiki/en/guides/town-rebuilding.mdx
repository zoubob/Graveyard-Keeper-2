## What Is the Town in Graveyard Keeper 2?

The sequel builds its economy around more than a graveyard. According to the game's official [Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/), rebuilding the Town and profiting from its residents through quests is one of the core pillars of the game — restoring a medieval settlement while commanding an undead workforce at the same time.

The community has already filled in the texture around that pillar. Players report that the town at the heart of the game is called Emberville, and that — unlike the small village hub of the first Graveyard Keeper — it finally really exists as a place you can rebuild building by building. Guide coverage makes the point with a chapter title that says it all: "The Town Finally Really Exists." Instead of a handful of static vendors, you get a settlement that visibly responds to your investment.

That shift changes how you think about progression. In the first game, the village was scenery you visited to buy and sell. Here, the town is a project — and the project pays you back.

## How Does Town Rebuilding Work?

No official documentation lists building costs or restore requirements yet, so treat any precise figure you see elsewhere with skepticism. What players report, though, is a consistent loop:

1. **Quests point you at the town.** Residents and city quests surface what the settlement needs next.
2. **Resources and labor close the gap.** You gather materials and put your undead workforce to work — the same zombies covered in our [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/).
3. **Restored buildings open more quests.** Each rebuild gives the town more life, and more life means more requests, more customers, and more profit.

The loop matters because it compounds. A restored building is not just cosmetic progress; community reporting suggests each step feeds back into the quest system that ultimately drives your income. If you want the broader picture of where this fits into your first in-game weeks, our [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) covers the early priorities that come before town work.

A practical way to frame the cycle:

| Stage | What you do | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Meet residents, pick up city quests | Reveals what the town needs |
| Supply | Gather materials, assign zombie labor | Turns graveyard output into town progress |
| Restore | Rebuild structures one by one | Unlocks more quests and residents to serve |
| Profit | Complete quests, trade with residents | The payback phase that funds the next rebuild |

## What Are City Quests and Why Do They Matter?

City quests are the bridge between town rebuilding and profit — that is the clearest way to describe the relationship that has emerged from early guide coverage. The official Steam description frames profiting from residents via quests as a core pillar, and city quests are the mechanism that makes it happen.

In practice, players report that quests tie the town's needs to your production lines. The town asks for things; you produce or restore; the town rewards you. Because the town grows as you rebuild it, the quest layer grows with it — which is why rebuilding and questing should be treated as one system rather than two separate chores. Breaking the loop in either direction stalls the other: a restored town with no questing earns nothing, and questing without rebuilding runs out of content.

For turning those rewards into a durable income, our [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide/) picks up where the quest chain leaves off.

## Who Are the Night Guards and What Do They Need?

Among the residents you will meet, community coverage of advanced tips highlights one group specifically: the town's night guards. The advice is short and memorable — feed the village night guards. Players report that keeping them supplied is part of keeping the town's rhythm running smoothly once you are active in it.

Exactly what the guards require, and what happens if you neglect them, is not documented publicly yet, so we will not guess at specifics. The safe play is the one the guides recommend: once you have unlocked the town and its guard presence, route food to them as part of your regular routine, the same way you keep your own pantry stocked. Food is the one resource this game never lets you stop producing, and the guards are one more mouth on the list.

## When Should You Start Rebuilding?

Earlier than you might expect. A recurring piece of advice in beginner-tip coverage — a bonus chapter in the community guide — is to unlock "city liberation" early. The reasoning tracks with everything above: the sooner the town loop starts, the sooner quests, residents, and rebuilding begin compounding in your favor.

That does not mean rushing it at the expense of your graveyard. The first in-game days still belong to your core setup — tools, energy, and your first zombies. But once the basics are stable, treat the town as your next milestone rather than a late-game afterthought.

A sensible order, based on community advice:

1. Stabilize the graveyard basics — energy, tools, and your first automation. The [guides hub](/guides/) keeps the full checklist.
2. Push for city liberation early, as the beginner-tip coverage recommends.
3. Keep the night guards fed and start picking up city quests.
4. Rebuild building by building, letting quest rewards fund each next project.

Follow that order and the town stops being a side feature and becomes what the official description says it is: one of the main engines of your economy.
