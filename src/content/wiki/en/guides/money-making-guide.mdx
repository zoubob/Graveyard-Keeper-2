Money in this game is a stacking problem, not a puzzle with a single answer. No one trick prints coin; income comes from overlapping loops that each remove a bottleneck, and the loops compound. One honest note up front: no specific price tables or earn rates circulate publicly, and inventing them would mislead you. This guide therefore describes loops and priorities — what to build, in what order, and why it pays.

## How Does Money Work in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Three layers, roughly in the order you unlock them. First, manual production loops you run yourself. Second, selling, which converts surplus into coin. Third, automation, which runs the loops without you. Stack sizes matter at every layer, because every sale and every resupply runs through your carry capacity — community guides flag selling and stack sizes as a tip in their own right, not a footnote. Guide creators sum up the sequel's whole economy as a profitable zombie apocalypse: manual labor funds the machines that eventually replace manual labor.

## What Is the Best Early Game Income Loop?

Wheat into bread. Community guides converge on the wheat-and-bread chain as the strongest early earner: both come from starter farming, the chain is short, and bread sells steadily. Pair it with wood. Prioritize wood early even though it feels like a chore resource — every workstation you plan will demand it, and a surplus sells while a shortage stalls every other loop you own.

| Loop | Why it earns | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wheat into bread | Short chain, starter tools, steady demand | Keep back enough seed and food stock |
| Wood and planks | Funds every build and sells as surplus | Stations will eat most of what you cut |
| Full-stack selling | Same goods, fewer trips, better margins | Stack sizes reward patience over impulse |

The quests that unlock the farming and crafting stations behind these loops are covered in the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/), and the [getting started checklist](/items/getting-started/) covers the tools each loop needs before it turns a profit.

## What Changes in the Mid Game?

Zombies do. Assign zombies as porters and equip them with tools, and the haul-everything-yourself phase ends — community guides treat this as the single biggest labor-cost cut in the game, because free labor that walks itself removes both the wages and the walking. The long-term shape of the economy is factories operated by zombie crews: automation at industrial scale replacing the grind you have been doing by hand. Which corpses become which workers is decided by the skull system, so it pays to read the [skull system explained](/zombies/skull-system-explained/) page before committing bodies. The [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) covers the workforce itself, from first minion to crew management.

## Can the Town Make You Money?

Yes — it is the official pitch, not just community theory. The game's own store description promises rebuilding the town and profiting from its residents via quests. In practice, community reporting frames the town as a slow compounder: the rebuilding investment front-loads early, and the quest-driven income from residents stretches across the rest of the run. Treat it as your mid-game savings account. Fund it once your wheat, bread, and wood loops are stable, and the [town rebuilding guide](/guides/town-rebuilding/) tracks the full chain of what unlocks when.

## What Should You Sell vs Keep?

Frame it as principles rather than a shopping list:

- Sell surplus in full stacks — a partial stack wastes the same trip
- Sell whatever your next planned unlock cannot consume
- Keep anything tied to upcoming tech tiers; a small sale now can fund an expensive rebuy later
- Keep unique components and quest-tagged items until you have confirmed they are not city quest material
- Keep production inputs for loops you intend to automate — zombies will need them more than the vendor does

Timing matters as much as the list. Because shops and events run on a weekly schedule, the best selling days are the ones your buyers actually appear — community guides recommend batching your surplus and hauling it on vendor days rather than selling piecemeal whenever a stack fills. Waiting also gives wheat, bread, and wood time to accumulate into full stacks, which is exactly what the rules above reward.

If you are deciding what to unlock next with your banked points, pair this page with the [tech points and mastery guide](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) so income and unlocks rise together.

## Is the Zombie Workforce the Real Money-Maker?

Over a full run, yes. Every community projection of the late economy lands in the same place: crews that work, haul, and produce while you do something else. But zombies are bought with corpses and setup time, and setup time is bought with early coin. The wheat field pays for the graveyard improvements; the graveyard produces the workers; the workers run the factories. The early loops are not a detour from the money-making — they are the down payment on it.
