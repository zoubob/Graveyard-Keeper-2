Graveyard Keeper 2 drops you into a graveyard with a corpse on the slab and almost no instructions. Community consensus is blunt about onboarding: the tutorial is considered the weakest part of an otherwise deep management game. This guide closes that gap with ten priorities drawn from the chapter structure of two community guide videos, both embedded at the end of this page. Everything here is community-reported rather than official, so treat it as direction, not doctrine.

## What Should You Do First in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Follow the quest line. Multiple guide creators describe quests as the gate for the game's core features, which quietly makes the quest log the real tutorial. Work the early quests in order and you will be introduced to farming, crafting, corpse handling, and the tech tree at roughly the pace the game intends. While you work through them, keep one basic production loop alive — the starter gear on the [getting started checklist](/items/getting-started/) is enough to fund your first real upgrades.

## Push Mastery Levels Early

Mastery is the widest unlock gate in the early game. Community guides put it at the very top of the priority list because so much of the map — tools, stations, late-game options — sits behind mastery thresholds rather than raw currency. Push your mastery levels deliberately in the first sessions, even where the immediate reward looks small, and the rest of the game opens faster. The [tech points and mastery guide](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) breaks down how the two systems feed each other.

## Why Does the Day of the Week Matter?

Because the world runs on a weekly schedule. Shops and events move on fixed weekdays, and players report losing hours by showing up to buy or sell on the wrong day. Learn the rhythm early and plan errands around it: stock up ahead of the days your vendors appear, and schedule long crafting sessions for the days when nothing can be bought anyway. The [day of the week calendar](/guides/day-of-week-calendar/) lists what happens when.

## Organize Storage Before It Swallows You

Inventory logistics earns its own chapter in community guides for a reason. Chests are cheap; clutter is expensive, because every hour spent digging through a mixed crate is an hour not spent producing. Set up dedicated storage early — one chest family per resource type, placed near where the resource gets consumed — and the habit carries you cleanly into the mid game when volumes explode.

## Bank Tech Points and Unlock in Batches

Spend tech points in planned batches, not one at a time. The community-recommended pattern is to bank them until an entire tier of unlocks is affordable, then buy the batch in one sitting. That avoids dead-end purchases, keeps production chains improving in coordinated jumps, and matches the mastery push above — unlocks land when you can actually build what they enable.

## What Do You Do With Unwanted Corpses?

Send them to the crematorium. Not every body deserves a plot, and graveyard quality suffers when you bury trash out of guilt. The skull system tells you which is which: community consensus is that white skulls serve the graveyard while red skulls are good for zombies. Sort every delivery before deciding, and burn what nobody wants. The [skull system explained](/zombies/skull-system-explained/) page walks through the details.

## Learn Zombie Basics Before You Need Them

Zombies arrive as a system long before they become your workforce, and early fundamentals matter. Two habits come up in every guide: equip tools on zombies so they can actually perform their jobs, and dedicate at least one as a porter so materials start moving themselves. Red-skull corpses make better workers, which is one more reason to sort bodies on arrival. The [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) covers the full progression from first minion to factory crew.

## How Do Faith, Sermons, and Waypoints Work?

Faith is the third currency, and sermons are how you farm it. Keep them stocked and delivered on schedule, because faith gates a surprising amount of progression. Community guides group waypoints into the same conversation: fast travel shapes how much of your week chores consume, so treat waypoint unlocks as infrastructure rather than convenience purchases.

## Sell Smart: Resources and Stack Sizes Matter

Selling is where your loops turn into coin, and stack sizes decide how efficient every trip is. The strongest early loops reported by players are wheat processed into bread, backed by a steady wood supply — prioritize wood early even though it looks like a chore resource, because every station you build will demand more of it. Sell in full stacks, keep the inputs your own production needs, and the [money making guide](/guides/money-making-guide/) expands these loops in detail.

## Eat Before You Run Out of Energy

Energy and sanity both fail quietly. Eat before the bar empties rather than after, and keep beer on hand — community guides specifically name it as the routine answer for controlling insanity. Mismanagement compounds: a bad day produces worse decisions, which produce worse days. The [insanity guide](/guides/insanity-guide/) explains the whole meter and its fixes.

## Take Your Time and Take Notes

This is a paced management game, and guide creators close their lists with the same advice: slow down. Keep notes on what unlocks when, which quests gate which features, and what next week's schedule looks like. Nothing in the early game punishes patience, but plenty punishes rushing.

### Bonus: Work Toward City Liberation Early

One bonus chapter from the community guides deserves its own line: unlock city liberation early. The town's rebuilding arc feeds into quests and residents later in the run, and starting it sooner compounds. The [town rebuilding guide](/guides/town-rebuilding/) follows the full chain.

## What Mistakes Waste the Most Time Early?

- Skipping the quest line and missing feature unlocks
- Building workstations without a layout plan — plan first, then build once
- Spending tech points one at a time instead of in batches
- Ignoring the weekly schedule and missing shop and event days
- Burying red-skull corpses that should have become workers
- Running energy to zero and losing days to recovery

None of these are permanent setbacks, but as of the v1.0 launch they are the six most common time sinks community guides warn new keepers about.
