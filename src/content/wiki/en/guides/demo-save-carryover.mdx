## Does Demo Progress Carry Over?

Pre-launch coverage of Graveyard Keeper 2 makes a specific and unusually generous promise: the free demo's progress transfers directly into the full game. One reviewer lists it among the things the studio delivered in a compressed run-up to launch — reveal, devlogs, a release date, a demo with carryover progression, and pre-orders with bonuses — and frames it as a deliberate choice rather than a marketing afterthought.

The framing in that coverage is worth quoting in spirit if not in words: a free demo is a good attitude, but a demo whose progress survives the purchase turns the experience into a genuine head start. Compatibility, in that reading, is a way of respecting the player's time.

Two things follow from that. First, the feature was described before release, not confirmed after it. Second, it was described by reviewers working from the demo and developer material, not from official documentation of the save system. Both facts matter, and they are why this page tells you to verify rather than promising you an outcome.

## Why Is Carryover a Big Deal in This Game?

Because Graveyard Keeper 2's opening is front-loaded with exactly the work nobody wants to repeat.

Pre-launch coverage names the specific chores: clearing land, gathering the initial sticks, and organizing basic tools. That is the part of a management sim where you are doing manual labor to build the conditions for interesting decisions, and in a game this step-heavy it can run for a real stretch of hours.

If that effort carries forward, the demo's value changes shape. It stops being a trial you discard and becomes the first chapter of the save you will actually play. That in turn makes the demo a genuinely low-risk way to answer the questions a prospective buyer has — how the controls feel, how the new three-dimensional presentation reads, how the loop sits with you — without paying for the answers twice. The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) covers what the opening hours actually contain, and the [getting started page](/items/getting-started/) covers the first decisions inside them.

## Is the Demo Available on Every Platform?

The same coverage reports a demo available on consoles as well as PC, and treats that breadth as significant. Console players in particular have less ability to judge a game from system requirements or a Steam refund window, so a playable demo is the only honest way to test performance before buying.

That coverage frames the demo's presence across platforms as mitigating part of the purchase risk — a reasonable reading, given that performance was that reviewer's single largest reservation about the game. If you are deciding between platforms, the [console versions guide](/guides/console-versions/) covers what is known about the different releases, and the [is it worth it guide](/guides/is-it-worth-it/) tracks the launch-status picture.

## How Should You Approach the Demo?

If the carryover works as described, the demo deserves to be played as a real save rather than a throwaway. That leads to a few practical adjustments:

- **Play it the way you would play the full game.** Sloppy demo habits are habits you keep if the save continues.
- **Do not chase content the demo withholds.** Coverage describes a limited area, and pushing at its edges wastes the hours you are trying to bank.
- **Sort your storage properly from the start.** The [inventory and storage guide](/guides/inventory-logistics/) exists because the mess compounds, and a carried-over save inherits the mess too.
- **Keep your expectations about scope modest.** A demo is a slice; the story beats it sets up continue after it ends, which the [Act 1 walkthrough](/guides/act-1-walkthrough/) covers.

That last point is the reason the demo works as a head start rather than a spoiler: it hands you a running economy, not a finished story.

## What Should You Verify Before Committing Hours?

This is the honest part. The transfer is described as an intended feature, and we have no documentation of how it is triggered, which platforms it covers identically, or what happens if the demo and the full game come from different storefronts. Rather than guess, check these on your own setup:

| Check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Demo exists on your platform | Coverage reports console demos, but storefronts change |
| Same storefront and account for both | Cross-storefront save transfer is not something any source confirms |
| Full game detects the demo save | This is the mechanic itself; test it before investing hours |
| Your platform's version is current | The demo and the game need to be on compatible builds |

Run a short test before treating the demo as your real save: play an hour, buy or install the full game, and confirm your progress appears. If it does, you have a head start. If it does not, you have lost an hour rather than a weekend — which is exactly the trade the verification is for.

## What Is the Honest Summary?

The offer, as reported before launch, is unusually player-friendly: a free demo, available broadly including consoles, whose progress is meant to survive into the full game. That is a strong reason to try before buying, and a strong reason to treat the demo as a real save.

The caveat is equally real. Everything above comes from launch-era coverage rather than official documentation, the mechanic's details are undocumented, and the correct posture toward any specific promise about your save is to test it yourself. Do that, and the demo becomes one of the better deals in the genre — a free slice of a game that is slow to start, with your slow start preserved.
