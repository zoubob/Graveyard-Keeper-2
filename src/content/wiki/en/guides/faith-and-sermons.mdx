## How Does Faith Work in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Faith is the sequel's third currency, sitting alongside coin and the tech points you spend on research. It is generated in the church, it is spent across several systems at once, and community beginner coverage makes a blunt claim about its early value: faith is tighter than money and matters more.

The reason is supply. How much faith you can generate per week is described as strictly linked to your progress through the story rather than to effort or grinding, so faith is a schedule you keep, not a resource you farm. Money you can always earn more of; faith arrives on the game's clock.

That makes the whole system a question of not wasting the windows you get. The [day-of-week calendar](/guides/day-of-week-calendar/) covers the week these windows sit in, and the [tech and mastery guide](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) covers the trees faith feeds.

## How Do Sermons Work?

Community coverage describes the rule simply: on a wrath day you are allowed to perform a sermon, and that is your faith income for the week. Miss the window and the income simply does not happen.

The important detail is what a sermon draws on. The sermon is linked to **town gratitude** — the standing your work in the town has earned. A sermon held with gratitude behind it pays accordingly; a sermon held with none pays poorly.

Practical setup reported by players:

1. Keep a working relationship with the town so gratitude accumulates.
2. Store the town-gratitude scrolls or stones you receive rather than spending them immediately.
3. When a wrath day arrives and you have not fulfilled gratitude in the preceding stretch, use the stored items to bring the sermon back up to a worthwhile level.

That third step is the whole trick. Stored gratitude is a buffer against a bad week, and coverage describes players banking them specifically so an under-delivered sermon can be topped up. If you are still building the town's trust in the first place, the [town rebuilding guide](/guides/town-rebuilding/) is the prerequisite.

## Does Church Quality Matter?

This is where the advice gets interesting and where you should be careful.

One community assessment is that church quality never does anything beyond determining the town-gratitude points available, and that at their stage of the game roughly 20 points was all the church needed. Everything past that — the decoration, the nicer fittings — was cosmetic, not mechanical.

Read that as community observation rather than a documented rule. No official source states a hard cap, and the threshold is described relative to a specific point in progression. What you can safely take from it is the shape: **church quality is a threshold you clear, not a number you maximize.** Clear the threshold your current stage needs, then spend the materials on the cemetery, where the return is better documented.

If you do invest in the church's surroundings, understand that you are buying appearance. That is a legitimate reason to build — it is just not the same reason as the next white skull. The [cemetery quality guide](/items/cemetery-quality/) covers where decoration does move a number.

## Which Prayer Should You Use?

Prayer type is named in community coverage as strongly influencing the reward, which makes it a lever worth understanding before your first sermon rather than after your fifth.

The honest limit: no public source publishes a prayer-to-reward table, so there is no ranking to hand you. What the guidance supports is a method — run one prayer type for a few sermons, note what comes back, then compare against another. Because faith income is limited to one sermon per week, the comparison takes real time, and that is an argument for settling the question early and then leaving it alone.

Crafting your own prayer at the scriptorium is recommended as an early goal by the same coverage, and it is the step that turns prayer choice from theory into a decision you control.

## What Else Does Faith Buy?

Two sinks are named as clearly worth it, and they are worth separating from everything else.

**Waypoints.** The Roman-numeral waypoints can all be unlocked, and unlocking them opens the map's locations. Community coverage is unusually direct here: go for them, because fast travel speeds up everything else you do afterward. The same coverage notes that the game repeatedly sends you back across the map for a couple of planks, which is exactly the trip fast travel deletes.

**An early quest.** Faith points are described as having a small role in the early quest line, and the recommendation is to hold roughly three in reserve for it. This is a cheap piece of insurance against arriving at a quest requirement with an empty pool and a week to wait.

Beyond those, faith flows into the inspiration trees. That is a genuine choice rather than a formality — the tree is described as vast — so spend the rest where your current bottleneck is rather than spreading points thinly. Beginner coverage's instinct is to clear the waypoints and the quest hold-back first, then commit.

## What Should You Not Do With Faith?

- **Skip a wrath day.** Your weekly income does not roll over. An unused window is faith you never see.
- **Let gratitude sit at zero.** A sermon with nothing behind it is the worst-value use of the one window you had.
- **Over-decorate the church before the cemetery.** Coverage treats the church's extra quality as cosmetic past the threshold; the cemetery's return is on better evidence.
- **Dump everything into the inspiration tree early.** Waypoints and the quest hold-back come first, and they are cheap.
- **Treat faith like money.** Money has many sources. Faith has one, and it runs on a calendar.
