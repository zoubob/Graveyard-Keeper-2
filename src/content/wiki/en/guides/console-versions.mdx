## What Platforms Did Graveyard Keeper 2 Launch On?

The launch was genuinely simultaneous. The official trailer states the game is available on PC — Steam, GOG and the Epic Games Store — plus PlayStation, Xbox and Nintendo Switch, with no staggered rollout between them.

That is a bigger logistical undertaking than the platform list makes it look. Community pre-launch coverage frames it as its single largest reservation: shipping across five platform families at once is a considerable challenge for a studio of Lazy Bear Games' size, and the coverage cites a pattern of simulation games that "stalled right on the Switch version." The stated reading was optimistic but watchful, with performance named as the variable keeping that coverage alert.

So the honest summary is: every major platform got the game on day one, and the version most worth scrutinizing before you buy is the one with the least headroom. The [performance guide](/guides/performance-settings/) covers why headroom is the question that matters for this particular game.

## Is There a Demo on Consoles?

Yes — and this is the most practical fact on the page. Pre-launch coverage reports a demo available on consoles as well as PC, and explicitly credits its presence on all platforms with mitigating part of the purchase risk.

The reasoning is sound for this game specifically. Console buyers have no system-requirements sheet and no refund window, so a playable demo is the only honest way to answer "does this run acceptably on my machine?" before spending money. Since the sequel rebuilt the world in three dimensions and carries a simulation load that scales with activity, that is not a trivial question.

If you have not yet decided, run the demo first. That advice is also the core of the [demo save transfer guide](/guides/demo-save-carryover/), which covers the reported carryover of demo progress into the full game — the detail that makes a console demo a genuinely cheap decision tool rather than a throwaway.

## Which Console Version Should You Choose?

There is no verified list of differences between the console releases in our sources — no resolution tables, no frame-rate comparisons, no feature parity notes. Nobody credible has published them, and inventing them would be worse than saying so.

What the available material supports is a decision process rather than a verdict:

1. **Run the demo on the platform you are considering.** This is the only measurement that reflects your hardware and your tolerance.
2. **Weight the Switch version most heavily.** It is the one the coverage singled out as a risk, on the strength of the platform's history with simulation-heavy games rather than any reported defect.
3. **Check current reports for your platform.** Launch-window performance is a moving target, and the [is it worth it guide](/guides/is-it-worth-it/) tracks the launch-status picture including the state of the game's technical polish.
4. **Decide on comfort, not on spec sheets nobody has published.** Portability, controller preference, and where your saves live are real factors; imagined performance differences are not.

If you are coming to the sequel from the original, the [what's new comparison](/guides/whats-new-vs-graveyard-keeper-1/) covers what changed, and the [story premise page](/guides/story-premise/) covers where the game starts.

## Does Your Platform Change Anything In-Game?

One thing, reportedly, and it is cosmetic.

Community coverage of the pre-order package describes an exclusive Grand Inquisitor outfit whose color changes with the platform you buy on. The listed colors are light blue for Steam, green for Xbox, a darker blue for PlayStation, and red for both Switch and Switch 2.

Two caveats before you let that influence a purchase. First, this is second-hand reporting — the coverage attributes the detail to material it found rather than to an official announcement, so treat it as community-reported. Second, if you are reading this after the pre-order window closed, the outfit may not be obtainable at all, since pre-order bonuses typically do not return.

The same coverage also describes a personal sauna among the pre-order items, framed as a humorous quality-of-life addition rather than a mechanic. Neither bonus is described as affecting gameplay balance.
