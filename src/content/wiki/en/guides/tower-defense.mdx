## What Is Combat Like in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Combat is the biggest structural addition in the sequel, and it is not bolted on as a side activity. The official Steam description confirms that you can craft armor and weapons and train troops, and that tower and fortification construction is a featured mechanic. Where the first game had only minimal combat, Graveyard Keeper 2 turns defense into a layer that runs alongside your graveyard and your production chains.

Community coverage of the pre-launch demo frames it as the moment the corpse routine gains a chaotic edge: alongside expanding, collecting resources, and turning the dead into workers, the direct defense of the village becomes a job of its own. Players report that you must prepare for hordes of aggressive zombies trying to destroy what you have built — a threat that community write-ups describe as creating a sense of urgency the first game practically lacked.

Treat that framing as demo-era reporting wherever it goes beyond the official feature list. The mechanics exist, but the fine detail is still settling as of the v1.0 launch.

## How Does Tower Defense Work?

The official description lists towers and fortifications as featured construction, and the community coverage fills in the intent. Players report that you can build towers, erect fortifications, and produce heavy weapons to hold off attacks, and that this changes what buildings mean: every wall and every building stops being decoration or visual progress and starts having real value when a horde appears. The summary from that coverage is that it turns your village into something that needs to be planned, protected, and reinforced over time.

That is the key shift for returning players. Building in the first game was largely about throughput — more stations, more storage, more output. Here, according to community reporting, building is also about survivability, and the sandbox, crafting, base-building, and tower-defense layers combine so that growing fast is not enough on its own.

## How Do Town Battles and Defense Power Work?

Launch-week coverage describes a central command location where you recruit battle squads and build fortifications, and it is where your first real fight begins. In that coverage the player enters the first battle against the zombies and calls it the beginning of the town's reclamation, placing crafted barricades rather than relying on a weapon. That matches the earlier reporting that defense is a construction problem as much as a combat one.

Battles also appear to have objectives beyond survival. The same coverage describes zombie spawn points tied to map areas, with capturing ground reported to remove a spawn point from the fight, and flags to capture during battles. If you have not yet started reclaiming the town, our [town rebuilding guide](/guides/town-rebuilding/) sets up the arc these battles belong to.

One concrete scouting figure from that coverage: an eastern-district town battle was reported to require one squad and 12 defense power, at a cost of 20 silver. That number is launch-week observation, not official documentation, so treat it as an example of scale rather than a fixed rule.

## How Do You Build an Undead Army?

The official description confirms troop training, and community coverage describes the concept plainly: you can train an undead army and lead it in battle, and the corpses that used to handle the property's strangest tasks can now become soldiers. The community framing is that you use and exploit the undead rather than only fearing them — the [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) covers that workforce from the ground up.

In practice, launch coverage shows the army being assembled through barracks slots. Barracks have squad slots, more slots can be unlocked, and the player reports using an emblem earned in combat to unlock one, then chaining four zombies into it. The reported achievement for crafting 50 battle items, and the crafting of extra bows at the anvil, both point to the army being a production problem: your zombie soldiers are equipped from your own crafting lines.

Because squads, barracks, and equipment are already covered in depth on our [squads and barracks page](/zombies/squads-and-barracks/), this guide stays with the combat layer itself.

## What Is the Best Defensive Setup?

The clearest community advice is not to rely on one unit type. Advanced-tips coverage describes combining rows of spike barricades with squads formed entirely of archer zombies: the barricades absorb the impact on the front line and halt the enemy advance, while the archers clear waves from a distance without exposing you. The stated lesson is that melee units alone cannot handle the job, and that mixing melee, ranged, and towers is what actually secures street combat.

The zombie guide's chapter list names zombie squads with barracks, pikes, and bows, which lines up with pikes and bows being documented equipment for your squads. So the community pattern is a holding line of fortifications, ranged squads behind it, and towers filling the gaps.

That said, exact damage values, tower types, and stat interactions are not publicly documented as of v1.0. Build for redundancy first, then optimize once you can measure your own results.

## When Do Town Battles Happen?

Community coverage suggests town battles are tied to a specific day-of-week timing, with at least one reported battle costing silver to begin. The exact schedule is not officially documented, so plan around it rather than trusting a fixed table. Our [day-of-week calendar](/guides/day-of-week-calendar/) tracks what is known about the in-game week and is the right place to check timing before committing to a fight.

For the story context these battles sit inside, the [Act 1 walkthrough](/guides/act-1-walkthrough/) covers the opening campaign. And if you are still deciding what to build first, the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) puts defense in its proper place in the early order — important, but not before tools and energy.

The honest bottom line: defense is real, it is central, and it rewards planning over reflex. The exact numbers are still being mapped by the community, so verify anything specific in your own save.
