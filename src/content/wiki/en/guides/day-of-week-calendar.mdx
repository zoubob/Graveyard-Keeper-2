## Does Graveyard Keeper 2 Have a Weekly Calendar?

Yes. As of the v1.0 launch, Graveyard Keeper 2 runs on a weekly calendar, and specific shops, events, and activities move on a day-of-week schedule. That single fact comes from the game's beginner-guide coverage — one video chapter titled "Tip 3: The days of the week matter" — and it is currently the only publicly confirmed detail about how the calendar works.

That may not sound like much, but it should change how you approach every session. If certain vendors or activities are only available on certain days, then when you play matters just as much as what you do. Two players with identical tech trees and identical zombie crews can have very different weeks depending on whether they plan around the calendar or ignore it.

The rest of this page explains why that matters, gives you a planning framework you can use today, and is completely honest about what is not yet verified.

## Why Do the Days of the Week Matter?

Weekly calendars are one of the oldest pacing tools in management sims, and this game is clearly built in that tradition. The pattern is familiar across the genre: gated availability forces decisions, and those decisions are where the game gets interesting.

- **Priority calls.** When a vendor or event appears on limited days, you decide each week what deserves your limited time and energy instead of doing everything eventually.
- **Batching.** Knowing that an opportunity recurs on a schedule lets you batch errands — craft, harvest, and stockpile before the right day arrives, rather than wasting in-game days wandering to a closed door.
- **Failure recovery.** A missed day is rarely a disaster. The calendar loops, so the cost of missing an opportunity is a week of patience, not a broken save.

Players report that this rhythm matters here just as it does across the genre: the weekly schedule shapes which activities are worth doing on which in-game day, and planning around it is what separates smooth runs from chaotic ones. The systems pages on this site — from [tech points mastery](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) to the [insanity guide](/guides/insanity-guide/) — all assume you are spending days deliberately, and the calendar is the grid those days sit on.

## How Should You Plan Your Week?

Until a verified schedule is published, use a planning framework rather than a data table. The template below is generic on purpose — the day labels are planning slots, not the game's confirmed weekday names, and no entry claims a specific vendor or event for a specific day.

| Slot | Planning focus | Habit to build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Set the week's goal | Pick one rebuild, research, or production target |
| Day 2 | Check gated availability | Look at what shops and events are open before spending energy |
| Day 3 | Batch production | Queue the crafting and harvesting that support your weekly goal |
| Day 4 | Town and quests | Handle city quests and errands that depend on other characters |
| Day 5 | Restock and sell | Convert surplus into money before the week closes |
| Day 6 | Buffer day | Absorb anything the week knocked off schedule |
| Day 7 | Review and prep | Note what next week gates, then pre-craft for it |

Two habits make this framework work regardless of the exact schedule. First, before you spend a busy in-game day on travel, check whether the person or event you actually need is available that day — a wasted trip is the most common calendar mistake in gated games. Second, keep a small stockpile of the goods you regularly trade, so a gated vendor never catches you empty-handed. Our [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) covers the stockpiling basics, and the weekly goal-setting habit pairs naturally with the sequencing advice in tech points mastery.
