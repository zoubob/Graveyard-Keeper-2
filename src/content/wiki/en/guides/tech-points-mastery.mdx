## What Do Tech Points and Mastery Levels Do?

Two currencies run your progression. Technology points are spent on the tech tree to unlock recipes, stations and upgrades. Mastery levels are a separate, wider gate — many unlocks simply require a certain mastery rank before the corresponding tech matters. Community guides describe them as parallel tracks that most new players conflate, usually to their own frustration.

| Track | What it is | What it gates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology points | Spent currency | Tech tree nodes — recipes, stations, upgrades |
| Mastery levels | Earned rank | A wide range of unlocks across the game |
| Zombie mastery | Earned rank, workforce side | How strong your zombies can become |

The tracks also move at different rhythms. Tech points accumulate as you play and only become progress when you spend them; mastery accrues steadily from playing at all. No exact point values or thresholds are public as of the v1.0 launch, so this page describes the loop and the priorities rather than numbers.

## Why Should You Push Mastery Levels First?

Because mastery gates the widest range of content. Community advice for the early game is blunt: push your mastery levels as early as you can, since a long list of unlocks is hidden behind mastery ranks no matter how many tech points you are hoarding. Ignore mastery and you end up holding currency for a tree you cannot open yet.

The compounding argument seals the ordering. Players report that leveling up your technologies compounds — earlier unlocks make later ones cheaper or faster to reach — so the sooner both tracks start moving, the faster the whole economy accelerates behind them. A player who delays mastery sits at a plateau that no amount of tech-point farming fixes, because the gate was never about points in the first place. Mastery first, tech second, is the sequencing that keeps both moving.

## How Should You Spend Tech Points?

In planned batches, not one-off unlocks. The most repeated [community tips video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WMkufDK7bKw) on this track is blunt: bank your tech points and spend them in deliberate batches rather than sprinkling them on whatever node catches your eye. Batch spending matters because the tech tree rewards commitment: a cluster of related unlocks serves a production goal, while the same points scattered widely serve nothing in particular.

A workable spending loop:

1. **Set a banking threshold.** Decide in advance roughly how many points you want banked before a spending session — the exact figure matters less than having one.
2. **Pick one production goal,** such as automating a single resource chain or upgrading one workshop line.
3. **Buy the cluster of nodes that serves that goal** and nothing else.
4. **Return to banking** until the next goal is clearly defined.

The discipline matters more than the specific threshold, because no published costs exist to optimize against as of the v1.0 launch.

## How Do Zombie Mastery Levels Differ?

They are a separate track with a separate purpose. Community zombie coverage dedicates a full chapter to Zombie Mastery Levels, describing them as a gate on how strong your workforce can become — your keeper's own mastery does not carry over to the undead side of the operation.

Practically, this means workforce investment has its own progression curve that never benefits from your personal grind. A player with maxed personal progression but untouched zombie mastery still fields weak workers, and the reverse holds just as firmly. If you are building toward a porter network or factory-scale automation, budget time for the zombie track explicitly instead of assuming your main-game rank covers it. The [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) walks that system in full, including Supply Station upkeep, organs and barracks squads.

## What Is the Fastest Early Progression Loop?

Combine the tracks deliberately: push mastery daily, bank tech points toward one production goal at a time, and keep the keeper functional so the loop never stops. The community framing is that progression here is a routine problem, not a grinding problem — the players who advance fastest are the ones whose days are structured.

A loop that respects all of it:

- **Morning:** work toward your current production goal so banked tech points keep accumulating.
- **Midday:** handle the self-care loop — energy, food, sleep — so insanity never interrupts the schedule.
- **Evening:** check the [day-of-week calendar](/guides/day-of-week-calendar/) for day-specific opportunities before planning tomorrow.

Two habits make the loop stick. First, do not let the keeper's condition slide, because a broken routine costs more time than it saves — the [insanity guide](/guides/insanity-guide/) covers the repair work in detail. Second, whenever progress stalls, check your mastery gap before anything else; most walls in the early game are mastery gates wearing a tech-point disguise. New players coming from the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) can adopt this loop from day one and never need to unlearn anything.
