Short answer: probably yes, if you like management games and can tolerate a weak tutorial and some launch jank. Long answer below, with the receipts separated from the vibes. Two ground rules for this review: every number here comes from the verified store listing as of September 26, 2026, and every opinion is attributed to the community members who hold it. One transparency note before we start — some early coverage was creator-sponsored, including one prominent 100-days challenge video its creator disclosed as sponsored by the publisher. This wiki has no publisher relationship, takes no money from sales, and gives no numeric score: just data, attributed consensus, and a reasoned verdict.

## What Is Graveyard Keeper 2?

The sequel to the graveyard-management cult hit, developed by Lazy Bear Games and published by tinyBuild, released September 22, 2026. You run a graveyard, process corpses, automate with zombies, and — new to this entry — rebuild a town, defend it, and command troops. It launched on PC through Steam, GOG, and the Epic Games Store, alongside PlayStation, Xbox, and Switch. Interface localization covers 11 languages including Simplified Chinese; English is the only full audio language.

The bundle is the notable one: at $23.18 it has been priced below the sequel's $24.99 list price while also including the entire first game, making it an obvious entry point if you have never played the original. Launch discounts expire, so confirm current numbers on [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/) before purchasing.

## What Do Players Say So Far?

The Steam rating is Very Positive, with 87 percent of 1,454 reviews positive as of September 26, 2026 — four days after launch. That is a strong opening for a sequel with an overhauled engine and a new genre layer, though early review counts always skew toward fans who bought on day one. The single most quoted framing from guide channels comes from Day9TV, whose coverage was titled Rough Tutorial, Great Game — and that phrase keeps resurfacing in community discussion because it matches the broader pattern: complaints cluster around onboarding and polish, praise clusters around depth and the new systems.

## What Are the Launch Weaknesses?

Two, and both are community-reported rather than universal. First, the tutorial: the onboarding is considered the weakest part of the game, leaving new players to discover systems the way the first game's players did — by failing at them. Second, performance: review channels and player discussions have raised launch performance issues, with experiences varying by platform and hardware. The practical takeaway is that opinions genuinely differ on buy-now versus wait-for-patches, and both positions are defensible depending on your tolerance.

## Who Should Buy It Now?

- Series fans — the consensus framing of a rough tutorial around a strong core is close to the ideal sequel pitch
- Management and automation players — the zombie-factory economy is the system community coverage praises most
- Newcomers at the bundle price — the Grave Deal prices game 1 plus the sequel below the sequel's list price alone
- Anyone with patience — players comfortable filling tutorial gaps with a [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) report few other blockers

## Who Should Wait?

- Players sensitive to performance problems — wait for confirmed patches if that describes your hardware or platform
- Players who bounce off bad onboarding — the tutorial will likely improve, and the game will still be there
- Deck or handheld players wanting verified stability — launch performance reporting is still settling
- Discount hunters — the 20 percent launch offer is not the floor this game will ever see

## How Is It Different From the First Game?

Briefly: 3D instead of 2D, tower defense and armies, Zombies 2.0 running factories, a town that exists and can be rebuilt for quest income, and a skull system that now splits corpse quality between graveyard use and zombie use. You play the same gravedigger, risen to Grand Inquisitor. ## The Verdict

We give no score, but we will give a position: Graveyard Keeper 2 is worth it for players who want a deep management game and accept that its launch tutorial and performance are works in progress. It is worth it especially at the bundle price for newcomers. It is a wait for players whose enjoyment depends on polished onboarding or verified technical stability. As of the v1.0 launch, the data supports both choices — which is itself the most honest thing a four-day-old game can say about itself.
