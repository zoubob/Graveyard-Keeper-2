## What Happens When a Corpse Arrives?

Every corpse delivery in Graveyard Keeper 2 is a decision, not a chore. Beginner coverage lists "corpse management and the crematorium" among its core tips for good reason: bodies are the game's most renewable valuable resource, and every one of them has a best use that is not automatically the use you first reach for. Let them pile up unsorted and you will bury corpses that should be working, work corpses that should be buried, and trip over a heap of bodies that serve nobody.

The decision is easier than it looks, because the game gives you a sorting key built into every body: the two-skull rating. White skulls measure how good a corpse is for the graveyard. Red skulls measure its potential as a zombie. One glance at the rating, one glance at your current needs, and the body's fate is settled. The [skull system explainer](/zombies/skull-system-explained/) covers that rating system in full — this page turns it into a routine.

## How Do You Sort Corpses by Function?

Advanced guide coverage is explicit about the principle: separate corpses by function. Two queues, two purposes:

| Sort | Destination | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong white skulls | Graveyard burials | Burial quality drives your graveyard rating, so the best bodies earn the best graves |
| Strong red skulls | Zombie production | High-potential bodies become elite workers through surgery and organ investment |
| Neither | Crematorium | Disposal keeps the pipeline clear and the yard usable |

The word "strong" is doing real work in that table. Community builds chase five-red-skull elite workers on the zombie side, and decoration-worthy burials on the graveyard side — both ends of the scale, not the mushy middle. Weak bodies in either queue drag down the thing they touch: a mediocre burial occupies a plot without earning its decoration, and a mediocre zombie occupies a job slot without performing it. When in doubt about where a body's ceiling lies, the [embalming guide](/zombies/embalming-and-red-skulls/) walks the hands-on craft of finding out.

## When Should You Use the Crematorium?

Advanced coverage compresses it into a phrase: "crematorium for the unwanted." The crematorium is not a failure state — it is the third queue, and it keeps the other two honest.

Burn bodies when:

- **The rating profile serves nothing.** Poor white skulls and poor red skulls on the same corpse mean no role exists for it.
- **Your queues are full.** A surplus of zombie candidates with no stations to staff them is just upkeep; the same goes for burial candidates with no free plots.
- **You need the pipeline moving.** Deliveries do not pause because your yard is cluttered.

New players often hoard bodies out of caution. The healthier habit is deciding fast: a corpse that serves no function today will serve no function next week either. The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) covers the early-game rhythm where those first deliveries arrive, and the [items getting-started guide](/items/getting-started/) covers the equipment you will want on hand before the yard gets busy.

## Does Surgery Quality Matter?

Yes — preparation is where a body's fate gets settled. Work done at the embalming table modifies skull ratings and, through harvested organs, drives the stats of the zombie you raise from the body. A rushed job leaves potential on the table; careful work is how the community produces its benchmark elite workers.

One community tip floats above the rest: one tips video reports that feeding a vegetable salad before surgery enables flawless operations. Present it the way the community does — as a tip to test, not a documented rule. The cost of trying it is a salad; the cost of believing it blindly is a ruined corpse if it turns out to be folklore.

## What Makes a High-Quality Burial?

On the graveyard side, advanced coverage points at decorations as the lever for cemetery quality: maximize the quality of each burial rather than merely filling plots. A decorated grave for a strong white-skull corpse earns more than an undecorated one for the same body — the plot is the same, the outcome is not.

That upgrades the sort decision, too. Cemetery space and decoration effort are finite, so they belong on your best bodies only. The compounding loop guides describe — quality burials lift the cemetery, a quality cemetery lifts your standing, your standing unlocks more of the game — starts with refusing to bury mediocre corpses in prime plots. Our [cemetery quality guide](/items/cemetery-quality/) works through the reported rating math, and the [organs guide](/items/organs-guide/) covers where the parts you strip out should end up.

## A Simple Daily Corpse Routine

Turn all of the above into a loop you can run on autopilot:

1. **Receive the delivery.** Check the skull ratings the moment the body lands.
2. **Sort immediately.** White toward the graveyard queue, red toward the embalming table, neither toward the crematorium.
3. **Prepare what needs preparing.** Shape bodies and harvest organs for zombie candidates; consider light preparation for burial candidates.
4. **Bury or burn.** Decorate the graves that deserve it; dispose of the rest without ceremony.
5. **Feed the workforce.** Route finished zombies toward porter duty or stations, per the [logistics network guide](/zombies/logistics-network/).
6. **Walk the yard once.** Clear clutter, confirm nothing has been sitting unsorted, and note what tomorrow's deliveries should become.

Run that loop every delivery day and the system takes care of itself — the [main zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) picks up where the routine leaves off.
