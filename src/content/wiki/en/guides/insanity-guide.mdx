## What Is Insanity in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Insanity is a mental-state mechanic layered onto the management loop. Alongside energy and hunger, the sequel now tracks your keeper's mind, and community guides treat keeping that meter settled as a routine task rather than a crisis. Neglect rest, food or comfort for long enough and the mechanic starts pushing back on everything else you are trying to do.

It is easiest to understand as the game formalizing something the first game left implicit: a keeper who never sleeps, never eats properly and never takes a break should not function well, and Graveyard Keeper 2 prices that neglect. Community tips videos devote full segments to controlling it, which tells you how visible the mechanic is in normal play — this is not a hidden system you can ignore until the credits.

No public numbers exist for thresholds or penalties as of the v1.0 launch, so everything specific on this page is framed as community-reported. The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) covers the surrounding early routines.

## What Happens When Insanity Gets Too High?

Community reports describe consequences rather than catastrophes. Players describe the mechanic as punishing a neglected keeper with degraded play — the details of exactly what degrades, and how harshly, are community-reported rather than documented, and no official threshold has been published anywhere.

The safest way to plan around it is to treat high insanity as a tax on everything else you are doing. Whatever else it interrupts, it interrupts the production routine the whole game runs on, and a broken routine is more expensive than the penalty itself. That framing also explains why the community's favorite mitigations are so mundane: they are routine repairs, not emergency items you hoard for a bad day.

If you want specific accounts of what goes wrong at high insanity, the community videos linked on this page are the best current record — just weigh their claims as player experience, not patch notes, and expect details to shift across patches.

## How Do You Reduce Insanity?

Two community-reported remedies dominate: beer and sleep. A [tips video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l2EXlN4NbWk) sums up the first as controlling insanity with beer, and its advanced-tips segment pairs sleep management directly against insanity — letting your keeper actually sleep, on a real schedule, keeps the meter settled.

That pairing is worth pausing on, because both remedies are things you already produce or already do. Beer is a brewing product, so reducing insanity doubles as a reason to run a production line you probably want anyway. Sleep is a scheduling decision, not an item at all — it costs discipline, not resources. Neither requires rare materials or deep tech-tree investment, which is why experienced players describe insanity as a routine problem rather than a resource problem.

The general principle underneath both: restore the keeper deliberately instead of letting the meter drift. Reacting only when something visibly breaks is how the mechanic goes from background noise to a lost week of progress.

## How Does Insanity Fit the Daily Routine?

As the third pillar of the self-care loop, next to energy and food. Community advice describes the trio as one system: eat before you run out of energy, sleep on a real schedule, and keep a comfort item like beer on hand. Handle all three and insanity becomes background bookkeeping; skip one and the other two start slipping with it.

A routine that keeps all three honest:

- **Eat before the tank is empty.** The community tip is to eat before running out of energy, not after — recovery is cheaper than collapse.
- **Sleep on schedule.** The advanced-tips framing is direct: manage sleep against insanity, rather than treating sleep as leftover time.
- **Keep beer stocked.** Brewing overlaps with the wider economy, so the remedy is also an industry with a market.
- **Anchor the schedule to the [day-of-week calendar](/guides/day-of-week-calendar/),** so day-specific work never eats the sleep window.

New players sometimes read self-care as lost work time. The community consensus runs the other way: a settled keeper puts in more productive days overall, and the [progression loop](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) only works if the keeper running it stays functional.

## Is Insanity Dangerous for New Players?

Only if you ignore it entirely. Your early routine is already busy, but the mitigations are cheap and available early — sleep costs schedule discipline, beer costs a modest brewing loop, and food is something you manage from the first week anyway. Community guides rank insanity as a mid-severity system: noticeable if neglected, trivial once it is routined.

The genuine risk window is the mid-game crunch, when large builds tempt you to skip sleep for several in-game days at a stretch. That is the scenario the mechanic seems designed to tax, and it hits hardest precisely when you least want an interruption. If you bank progress in planned pushes and keep the self-care loop intact between them, the danger never really materializes. Stock comfort supplies from the [items overview](/items/getting-started/) before any big push, and treat a well-rested keeper as part of the build cost.
