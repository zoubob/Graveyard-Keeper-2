## How Do Tools Work in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Tools do two jobs in Graveyard Keeper 2: the ones you swing yourself, and the ones your zombies wear. The second job is the one that trips people up. Community beginner coverage reports that zombies are born with zero mastery, and "without that stat, the game won't even let them touch the production queue" — they just stand at the station, idle, until you fix the problem.

The game gives no warning when something is wrong. A zombie with the wrong tool does not complain or flash an error; it simply remains idle. Coverage sums the rule up as treating "every zombie like an employee who needs the right equipment to do the right job." If a station looks staffed but nothing happens, check the tool first.

Context for what follows: Lazy Bear Games and tinyBuild released Graveyard Keeper 2 on 2026-09-22 at $24.99 on Steam (App ID 4358690). Everything below the official line is community-reported from launch-window coverage, since the game ships no public tool stat sheet.

## Which Tool Does Each Zombie Need?

The rule is function-matching: the tool must match the job you want that zombie to perform.

| Zombie job | Tool to equip | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wood cutting | Axe | Unlocks chopping work |
| Carpentry bench | Bronze axe (reported) | Enough mastery to start at once |
| Hammering bronze nails | Hammer | Matches the station's function |
| Any production station | Tool matching that station | Wrong tool means an idle zombie |

Community coverage gives the axe as the clearest example. A bronze axe "provides a good chunk of mastery right away, enough for them to start chopping wood or working at a carpentry bench without delay." A hammer covers the stations that want hammering, such as producing bronze nails. Match the tool to the function and the zombie starts; mismatch it and you get a body standing in a yard.

The practical habit is to keep a stock of tools on hand, so you are not crafting one every time you raise a worker. Beginner coverage recommends building up bronze tools early so new zombies can start immediately instead of waiting on a craft queue. For how workers slot into your operation, see the [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) and the [logistics network guide](/zombies/logistics-network/).

## How Much Does Tool Quality Actually Change?

The community numbers get specific here, and you should read them as reports rather than documentation. Advanced-tips coverage describes upgrading from rusty to bronze tools:

- "The rusty tool gives only about plus one work mastery."
- "With a bronze one, the zombie already gains plus two or more."
- That increase "can allow them to execute better recipes besides working faster."

The same coverage describes continuing the upgrade path: "As I get superior tools, like iron ones, I also upgrade the zombies." So the shape of the ladder, as far as community reporting goes, runs rusty to bronze to iron, with each step reportedly raising the mastery a zombie contributes and potentially widening the recipes it can run.

Two honesty notes: these figures come from a single piece of advanced-tips coverage and are not cross-confirmed, so treat the exact mastery values as unverified. And the low tier is described as "rusty" in the coverage; if your in-game item reads slightly differently, the point still holds — rusty tools, as the coverage puts it, "only hold down the basics until you can get something better."

## Should You Craft More Tools or Raise More Zombies?

Advanced-tips coverage offers a habit for every hiring decision: "Before thinking I need more workers, I check if the ones I already have are equipped to perform better." A zombie with a bronze tool is often worth more than two with rusty ones, and easier on your corpse supply than raising a second body.

That logic connects to a known constraint: zombie-guide coverage reports that oversized hordes carry penalties, so adding bodies has a cost beyond the corpse. Upgrading the tools you own sidesteps that entirely. Related: coverage notes that bronze collars — the first collar tier — lock a zombie at five red skulls, so early collar and tool decisions both shape what a worker can become.

1. Equip every working zombie with at least a rusty tool so none sit idle.
2. Match each tool to that zombie's station before raising anyone new.
3. Replace rusty tools with bronze as soon as you can craft them.
4. Check existing workers' equipment before expanding the workforce.
5. Move to iron when you can, and keep the upgrade habit going.

## What Should a New Player Do About Tools?

Treat tools as part of onboarding a zombie, not an afterthought. Beginner coverage is clear that equipping bronze-tier or better unlocks a zombie's labor — an unequipped zombie is a body standing in a yard. Multi-source reporting agrees: give a worker the right tool and the queue opens; leave it empty-handed and nothing happens.

So the early checklist is short: build a bronze tool as soon as you can, hand it to your first worker, and match tool to function as you add stations. Then keep upgrading — rusty to bronze, bronze to iron. The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) places tools in the first-days order, and the [items overview](/items/getting-started/) shows how the tool group fits beside food, organs and resources.

One last honest note: the ladder above iron is not documented as of v1.0. Work from your tech tree, verify in your own game, and treat every number here as community-reported rather than official.
