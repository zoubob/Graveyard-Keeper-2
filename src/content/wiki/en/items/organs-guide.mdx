## What Are Organs Used For in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Organs are the resource that connects Graveyard Keeper 2's two halves: the burial business above ground and the zombie workforce below it. You pull them from corpses during preparation, and community coverage of the zombie system describes them as what drives your undead workers' stats. Harvest the right parts and you build a stronger minion; harvest carelessly and you waste a body that might have funded a grave.

Community coverage treats organs as a full subsystem rather than a footnote — one zombie guide devotes a whole section to "Improving Your Zombies: Organs & Stats." Nothing about organs is spelled out in official materials, though, so read everything below as community-reported rather than developer-documented.

## Where Do Organs Come From?

Every corpse you process is a parts bin. Community coverage describes the flow like this:

| Step | What happens | Community-reported purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corpse preparation | You open the body and inspect its ratings | Read white skulls (burial) vs red skulls (zombie) |
| Harvest | Extract organs and bones | Feed zombie stats, or shape a future body |
| Route | Decide grave body vs zombie body | Keep cemetery bodies clean |

That routing decision is the whole game here. A high-white-skull corpse wants the ground; a high-red-skull corpse wants the workbench. The [skull system explained](/zombies/skull-system-explained/) breaks down how to read those two ratings before you cut, so start there if the vocabulary is new.

## How Do Organs Shape Red Skulls?

Community coverage of advanced tips reports that organs do not just sit inside a body as passive stats — they can be extracted and reinserted to change how many red skulls a corpse carries. The reported beginner path is specific: keep the vital organs and the fat intact, and extract only the bones first, because that "easily generates the modifiers you need."

Those extracted bones are not trash. The same coverage reports you can reinsert them into other bodies to "gradually build, piece by piece, the perfect minion." In other words, organs are a transferable resource between corpses, not a one-way loot drop, and one good body can donate to several. Exact point values for how much each organ moves the rating are not publicly documented as of the v1.0 launch, so treat the direction as reliable and the arithmetic as unconfirmed.

## How Many Red Skulls Should You Chase Early?

There is a reported ceiling worth knowing before you over-invest. Community coverage states that bronze collars, the first tier you get, "lock the zombie at five red skulls." If that holds in your build, pushing a single body past five red skulls early buys you nothing until you have better collars — which means the practical early goal is to build your best five-red-skull workers first and worry about going higher only after the collar tier improves.

This matches the community-converged target elsewhere: five red skulls is treated as the elite-worker benchmark. The [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) covers zombie creation end to end if you have not built your first worker yet.

## Should You Put Bad Organs Into a Body?

Yes — but only into the right body. One of the sharper tips in the community coverage is about routing: keep the bad organs precisely to put into the corpses that are going to become zombies, because it makes no sense to stain a good cemetery body with a corrupted organ.

That is a routing rule, and it neatly mirrors the skull logic. Low-quality or corrupted parts go into corpses destined for the workforce; cemetery-bound bodies are kept clean so their burial rating is not dragged down. The [cemetery quality](/items/cemetery-quality/) article covers the burial side of that math in detail, and it is the reason you should sort organs by destination rather than dumping every part into the nearest body.

## What About the Embalming Table?

The embalming table sits between raw organs and a finished worker, and the zombie guide devotes a chapter to it — "The Embalming Table & Red Skulls" — which community coverage ties to strengthening a body's red-skull count. We cover that process on its own page in [embalming and red skulls](/zombies/embalming-and-red-skulls/), so this article stays on the raw-material side: where organs come from and where they should go.

## Where Do Unwanted Corpses and Organs Go?

Not every body deserves a grave or a workbench. Community coverage reports that the crematorium becomes available after unlocking body places, and that cremating a body still pays you the burial certificate plus ash and salt that are usable in alchemy. For corpses that would waste either a cemetery slot or a zombie slot, that is the honest disposal route.

The full corpse pipeline — intake, sorting, cremation — is mapped in the [corpse management guide](/guides/corpse-management/), which is the better page to read once you are juggling multiple bodies at once.

## What Should You Do First With Organs?

A simple order to start with, based on the community-reported tips above:

1. **Read the body before cutting.** Check its white versus red skulls.
2. **Set the good bodies aside.** Keep the ones worth investing in for a real build.
3. **Extract bones first on early experiments.** Keep vital organs and fat intact, per the reported beginner path.
4. **Reinsert into your target body.** Build the perfect minion piece by piece.
5. **Route the bad organs into zombie-bound corpses only.** Never into a cemetery body.

Early on, messing up a good body costs a lot, which is why the coverage advises experimenting on ordinary corpses and saving the strong ones. Organs are forgiving precisely because they move — a part pulled from one body can rescue another, and nothing you harvest is wasted if you route it to the right destination.
