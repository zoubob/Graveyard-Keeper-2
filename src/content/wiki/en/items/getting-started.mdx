## What Items Matter Most in Graveyard Keeper 2?

Graveyard Keeper 2, developed by Lazy Bear Games and published by tinyBuild, spreads its item landscape across a handful of broad groups, and each group feeds a different part of your operation: work tools, food, organs, cemetery decorations, raw resources, and — new for the sequel — military equipment. You do not need to master every group at once, but knowing what each one is for keeps early spending honest.

Quick orientation before we go deeper:

| Group | What it does | Who it matters to first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work tools | Equip on zombies to unlock their labor | Automation-focused players |
| Food | Restores energy; supports surgery work | Everyone, from day one |
| Organs | Harvested from corpses; drive zombie stats | Midgame optimization |
| Decorations | Raise cemetery quality | The burial business |
| Resources | Sold, stacked, and stockpiled | Your income pipeline |
| Military gear | Armor, weapons, fortifications | The town-defense layer |

## Which Tools Should You Upgrade First?

Tools split into two jobs in this game: the ones you swing yourself, and the ones your zombies wear. Community coverage of advanced tips highlights the second job as the one most players miss — equipping bronze-tier or better tools on your zombies is what unlocks their labor. An unequipped zombie is a body standing in a yard; a zombie with the right tool is a worker.

One honest caveat: the full tool tier ladder beyond bronze is not yet publicly documented, so we cannot tell you exactly what sits above bronze or what each rung requires. Work from what your tech tree shows in-game, and prioritize the tools your current production bottleneck actually needs — the sequencing advice in [tech points mastery](/guides/tech-points-mastery/) helps you order those unlocks instead of buying whatever looks shiny. Our [tools guide](/items/tools-tier-list/) collects what is actually confirmed about the tiers and which zombie needs which tool.

## What Food Should You Keep Stocked?

Meals restore energy, which makes food the fuel for everything else you do — every swing, craft, and burial costs stamina that the kitchen gives back. Keep a steady rotation of simple meals running and you will rarely lose an in-game day to an empty bar.

One community-reported tip stands out for surgery work: players report that a vegetable salad, fed before operating, enables flawless surgeries. As with anything community-sourced, treat it as a strong lead rather than scripture — but because corpse preparation is the heart of the game's economy, keeping salads on hand before you cut costs you almost nothing. Corpse handling ties directly into the [skull system](/zombies/skull-system-explained/), which shapes what a body is ultimately worth.

## What Do Organs Do?

Organs are harvested during corpse preparation, and they are far more than loot to pile up. Per our [zombie guide](/zombies/zombie-guide/) — specifically its chapter on improving your zombies — organs are what drive zombie stats. The quality of what you pull out of a corpse shapes how well the undead worker you build from it performs.

That creates a decision point every time a body arrives on your slab: harvest it for parts, or process it for burial profit. The [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide/) walks through the profit side of that choice in detail. The short version: organs feeding your zombie workforce is often what makes automation cheaper later, so do not reflexively sell every harvest.

## How Do Decorations Affect Cemetery Quality?

Burial decorations are the community-noted lever for maximizing cemetery quality. Coverage of advanced tips frames decorations as the way to push a cemetery from functional to excellent — and cemetery quality is what the burial business is judged on, which makes decorations an income item as much as a cosmetic one.

The practical takeaway is sequencing. Decorations compete with tools, buildings, and tech for the same early resources, so most players report treating them as a parallel investment: upgrade the graveyard's presentation as your income allows rather than all at once. A steady drip of decoration work keeps quality climbing without starving the production lines that fund it.

## What Military Equipment Exists?

This group is official rather than community-reported. The game's [Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/) confirms that you can craft armor and weapons, and train troops, alongside tower and fortification construction — a military layer the first game never had.

On the equipment side, zombie-guide coverage notes that pikes and bows can equip zombie squads, meaning your undead workforce can double as an armed one. How deeply that meshes with tower construction and the town's defense is still coming into focus as of the v1.0 launch, so we will keep this section updated as the community maps the full military tree.

## Where Should New Players Spend Their First Resources?

Community beginner tips converge on a short list. Wood is called out as the early-game priority, because it feeds the constructions everything else depends on. Stack sizes matter too: selling surplus instead of hoarding every resource keeps your storage honest and your wallet growing, which is the loop the whole economy runs on.

A reasonable first-spending order, based on that coverage:

1. Basic tools for yourself, then bronze-tier tools for your first zombie.
2. Kitchen capacity, so energy stops being the bottleneck.
3. Wood and core constructions before luxury upgrades.
4. Decorations and military projects once the core loop is stable.

The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) covers the full first-days plan if you want the complete sequence.
